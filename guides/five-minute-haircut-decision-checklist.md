# Before your next haircut: a five-minute decision checklist

*A practical, free guide from FitMyFace. Published November 10, 2026. For adults 18+ outside Illinois.*

A picture of a haircut is useful, but the finished result also depends on your natural hair, the person cutting it, styling time, and the exact shape you request. Before you commit to a new cut, try this five-minute check.

## Minute 1: Decide what you want to **keep**

Pick two things that matter more than a style name:

- **Length:** what is the shortest you would accept at the front, sides and back?
- **Time:** how many minutes are you willing to style it on a normal morning?
- **Upkeep:** would you return for frequent trims, or do you need a style that grows out easily?
- **Texture:** do you want to keep your natural waves, curls, coils or straight texture?

You do not need to know haircut terminology to answer these questions.

## Minute 2: Compare two *specific* choices

Don't ask only “would short hair suit me?” Compare **two actual cuts**.

For example:

| Decision | Choice A | Choice B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front | Longer fringe across forehead | Short fringe off forehead |
| Sides | Longer, softer outline | Tapered, cleaner outline |
| Styling | Can air-dry | Needs regular product and shaping |
| Risk | Retains more length | Bigger change that takes time to grow out |

If you're stuck between similar looks, compare the **difference that affects daily life**, not which photo looks best under studio lighting.

You can [preview hairstyles on your own photo with FitMyFace](https://fitmyface.pages.dev/?src=medrepo). Start with an instant look or an AI-assisted preview if capacity is available. This is an *illustration*, not a prediction of an exact professional haircut or its suitability.

## Minute 3: Write a “do not do” instruction

This protects against the biggest avoidable misunderstanding.

Examples:

- “Please do not cut the front shorter than my eyebrows.”
- “Keep enough length on the sides to tuck behind my ears.”
- “I want layers, but no drastic thinning.”
- “Please confirm the shortest length with me before cutting.”

Replace those examples with your own constraints. A clear boundary is often more useful than a celebrity reference image.

## Minute 4: Prepare the conversation with your barber or stylist

Bring **two views** if you can: what you want, and the part you do *not* want changed. Then ask:

1. How would this work with my actual density, texture and growth pattern?
2. What would it look like without styling products?
3. Which part requires frequent maintenance?
4. Could we cut conservatively first, check, and go shorter later?

For help turning the idea into specific instructions, read the [FitMyFace barber-communication guide](https://fitmyface.pages.dev/guides/what-to-tell-your-barber).

## Minute 5: Make the reversible choice first

If you're unsure, start with the change you can reverse fastest: parting, styling, glasses, or a small trim. Hair length takes time to return. You can keep two preferred looks and bring them to a professional instead of demanding a one-shot makeover.

**Special cases:** Curly, coily and textured hair can shrink substantially from wet to dry; ask how the stylist will check final dry length. For glasses, an image preview cannot confirm bridge fit, pressure or lens suitability; actual dimensions and in-person fitting still matter.
